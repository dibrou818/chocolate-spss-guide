Do the whole project in SPSS, one click at a time

This page follows the “Course methods only” solution, but slows every step down to single clicks. Before each step you see the SPSS window as it should look when you click OK. After each step you see the result that SPSS will give you. We ran every test on your dataset beforehand, so you know in advance which conditions pass, which fail, and what to do when one fails.

How to read the pictures

The grey boxes are drawings of SPSS windows, not screenshots. A 1 red number gives the order of the clicks. A yellow box with a red border is what you click or fill in. Grey crossed-out text is something you must not tick. The white tables with a grey frame are drawings of the SPSS output, with the cells you must read in yellow.

Your numbers can differ a little

SPSS shows 3 decimals; this page mostly shows 2. A difference in the last decimal is normal. A bigger difference (for example 616 valid cases instead of 617) means a step went wrong: each step has a “If you see … → do …” table to find the cause.

The assignmentWhat the instructions ask, and where each item is on this page

We re-read the 3 documents (Instructions, Group project slides, Survey). Background: a chocolate maker launched a healthy chocolate (same taste, added antioxidant). It wants to target it by health concern, measured by guilt, shame and lack of joy after eating chocolate (survey Q16, g1–g5). You first build 3 segments: (1) high, (2) medium, (3) low health concern. Then you answer 6 research questions on the 691 Belgian consumers.

1. Prepare the dataset (before any RQ)

  • Names, labels, values (all codes for categorical, end-points for interval), measures → A1
  • Impossible values → missing → A2 (plus household size → A3)
  • Summated scales: feelings (Q2), self-control (Q14), health concern (Q16); check α (or Pearson r with 2 items: not needed here, all scales have 3+ items), then actually compute the scale → A4
  • Split health concern into 3 groups → A5

2. For every RQ: the 7 analysis steps

  • 1 whole dataset or subset · 2 IV/DV and measurement levels · 3 test(s) · 4 assumptions · 5 extra info (means, new variables) · 6 Select Cases and switch back · 7 full reporting with graph
  • Each RQ section starts with these 7 steps already filled in → RQ1 … RQ6
  • “Analyse the entire test”: all post hoc pairs, all DVs, M and SE, effect sizes, not only what answers H1.

  • Max 15 pages (graphs included, appendix excluded), structured per RQ, all 6 RQs solved
  • Screenshot of the full Variable View; impossible values, new variables, α reported (report sentences in A2–A5)

  • Statistics written as in class (sentences, symbols, decimals) + a graph per RQ + recommendations for the product manager (green boxes per RQ)

2

Find your results in the Output window

  1. After OK, SPSS opens (or brings to the front) the Output window. On the left there is an outline (a tree). Click a title in the tree to jump to that table.
  2. Each analysis adds a block at the bottom. Scroll down or click the last title in the tree.
  3. Tables are titled with the variable label, not the name. After you label health “How concerned people are about their health after eating chocolate”, its frequency table has that sentence as title.
  4. “.000” in a Sig. column means p < .001. Never write p = .000: write p < .001.

One-Sided p column: do not use it

SPSS 27+ also prints a One-Sided p (,027 for self-control, which would be “significant”). The course rule is to read the Two-Sided p. Self-control is therefore not significant (p = .054). Say it is close to the limit: the direction (target group resists less) matches the brand idea, but the evidence is not strong enough.

Report sentence

Levene's test was significant for attractiveness (p = .002), so equal variances were not assumed. People who eat chocolate to kill hunger between meals (M = 5.78, SE = .12) find chocolate significantly more attractive than other people (M = 5.49, SE = .07; t(184.21) = 2.02, p = .045). The difference is small (d = .18). H1 is supported. They do not find chocolate significantly harder to resist (M = 4.01, SE = .17 vs M = 4.36, SE = .07; t(689) = −1.93, p = .054), so H0 is not rejected for self-control.
